## Keyframe Control of Smoke Simulations【1】

I'm writing to review the (maybe the most) classic differentiable simulation paper.

## Forward Problem

$$
q_0 \xrightarrow{S} q_1 \xrightarrow{S}q_2 \xrightarrow{S}\cdots\xrightarrow{S}q_T
$$

### Initial State $q_0$

Forward simulation problem had been done well then (2003).

Given initial states $q_0$ including

* initial density $\rho$,
* initial velocity $\mathbf{v}$,
* temperature $T$,
* buoyancy $B$,
* viscosity $\mu$,
* boundary condition,
* external force $\mathbf{F}$,

a forward simulator tells the succeeding motion of the smoke.

However, in actual animation production, there is a big problem:

> A forward simulator is good at telling you what will happen, but not good at running as you want.

This is exactly what is called ***Inverse Control*** / ***Optimal Control***.

## Keyframe Control

The most important part of the paper is deadly simple.

First define simulation state $q_t$

$$
q_t=(\rho_t,\mathbf v_t)
$$

control parameters $u$

$$
u=\{u_0,u_1,\dots,u_{T-1}\} 
$$

Here, the control parameters $u$ can be viewed as several ***Wind Force Fields*** exerted on the system.

Now, the simulation becomes

$$
q_{t+1}=S(q_t,u_t)
$$

$$
st. \quad \rho_{t_k}\approx \rho^*_{t_k}.
$$

with a loss term

$$
\mathcal L(u)
=
\underbrace{
\sum_k
D(\rho_{t_k},\rho^*_{t_k})
}_{\text{match target}}
+
\lambda
\underbrace{
R(u)
}_{\text{minimal control force}}.
$$

The final problem goes as

$$
u^*=\arg\min_u \mathcal L(u).
$$

## Towards Differentiable Simulation

Now we have the loss term $\mathcal L(u)$. There is a natural question we have to tackle with: How to get the $\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial u}$ term?

### Differentiate the program, not an idealized equation.

The major idea behind the paper when calculating the $\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial u}$ term is

> **Not to calculate the analytical derivatives, but the actual numeric ones.**

Even if analytic derivatives are calculatable, these values are not always (or always not) the true derivatives that work for the optimization. So Treuille et. al. address that

> **"Solver could be not so precise, but the gradient must be consistent with the corresponding numeric solver."**
