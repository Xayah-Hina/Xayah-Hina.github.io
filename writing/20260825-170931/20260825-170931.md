This article is written to answer a crucial question about physics-based controlling problems:

***Why must we apply physics-based methods, rather than procedure-based ones, to achieve similar effects?***

## Problems in procedure-based methods

For a simplest case, in order to form a "S M O K E" smoke shape, we may simply apply

$$
\rho(x,t) = (1-\alpha(t))\,\rho_{\text{plume}}(x,t) + \alpha(t)\,\rho_{\text{text}}(x)
$$

![smoke](./a17b79eb9924dd46a386fd5ebf9216e63a2ecda2240e028da4d8103854a6c8ea.png "smoke")

However, of course, this pure procedure-based method would look unnatural and abrupt in most case.

But another question still exists: is physical one ensures to be natural?

## Stress test for controlling nonlinear PDE system

A common question we may have is that: making smoke plumes to form words "S M O K E", isn't that itself weird?

Things are in fact the other side. This is merely a stress test, or researchers designed this task deliberately to test the effectiveness of algorithms.

If a task is designed like from a natural plume shape to another natural one, it would be hard to identify whether these algorithms are taking effects.

## Edges in physics-based methods: Limit search space

Let's discuss the essence of the question we mentioned above.

First, define the whole solution space of a smoke animation:

$$
\mathcal A = \{\rho(x,t)\}.
$$

The procedural methods allow to draw extremely freely. However, most of them are absolutely impossible states for natural plumes.

For example:

* mass not conserved
* obstacle penetration
* time inconsistency
* ...

In other words, the actual valid dynamics trajectories are only a small subset:

$$
\mathcal M_{\text{physics}} \subset \mathcal A.
$$

which could be seen as a physical trajectory manifold.

Therefore, the actual edge in physics-based methods is not for "**generating animation**", but "**limiting search space**".

In other words, we may view it as **physical priors** for the whole optimization problems.
